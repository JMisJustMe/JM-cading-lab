# 3. Two-character battle law

Reducing the field from four fighters to two is a **scope reduction**, not a quality reduction.

Each side begins with two distinct fighters. Both must participate in battle through a combination of:

- active embodiment;
- live assist;
- tag/swap;
- cover/intercept;
- shared route or linked state;
- duo chain;
- rescue/recovery;
- partner vulnerability;
- duo finisher or broken-duo consequence.

The ordinary readable state may feature one primary and one partner, but the partner cannot disappear into passive inventory. Every exchange must preserve a meaningful question about both bodies.

Minimum relation route:

**SELECT DUO → SET RELATION → AIM/ENACT → PRIMARY CONTACT → PARTNER RESPONSE → RIVAL ANSWER → STATE/FORMATION CHANGE → RECOVERY OR FINISH → TRACE**

# 4. Three-manner law

## Flick Clash

Physical heart:

**TOUCH → PULL → AIM → CHARGE/FORCE → RELEASE → REBOUND/COLLIDE → CONSEQUENCE → RECOVER**

## Clash Flow

One-hand assisted enactment. Assistance may carry locomotion or route completion, but the player still owns target, tactic, timing, partner relation, risk and consequence.

## Clash Manual

Direct two-thumb enactment. Movement, aim, defence, ability and partner action remain embodied rather than reduced to menu selection.

## Shared-depth requirement

Across all three manners, the following remain the same combat truth:

- fighter identity;
- duo relation;
- HP and damage;
- states;
- arena laws;
- abilities;
- win/fail conditions;
- progression;
- trace.

The manner changes **how the player carries the action**, not whether the action has depth.

The coding bodies serve the game. They do not become competing products or visible architecture homework for the player.
