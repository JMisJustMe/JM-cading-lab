## Theodore's fighting-game definition

A fighting game is not limited to boxing, punching or humanoid martial arts.

Within the JM estate, a fighting game is a governed PvP body in which opposing sides directly pressure, outplay, damage, displace, trap, exhaust, outscore, deny, capture or defeat one another through meaningful action and counter-action.

Therefore these may all be fighting games:

- Smash-style arena combat;
- T-Boys;
- THREE MINUTE DUELS;
- Castle Duels;
- card battles;
- tactical placement battles;
- future word and Scrabble-like combat bodies.

The genre test is not **does somebody punch?**

The genre test is:

> **Do opposing bodies meaningfully fight for advantage through attack, defence, pressure, answer, reversal and defeat?**

## Control correction

Aiming belongs inside the fight.

Drag, aim, flick and release may carry:

- lunges;
- projectiles;
- grapples;
- passes;
- launch attacks;
- curved attacks;
- partner assists;
- knockback direction;
- environmental interactions;
- finishers.

They do not define all locomotion, all contact or all damage.

The complete combat route is:

**MOVE → FACE → ENGAGE → ATTACK → DEFEND → EVADE → COUNTER → ASSIST → TAG → CHAIN → KNOCKDOWN → RECOVER → PRESSURE → FINISH → TRACE**

## Damage and health law

Health is lasting body condition.

Guard/Poise is recoverable defensive stability.

- guarded attacks primarily damage Guard/Poise;
- clean hits damage Health;
- counter-hits, wall contact, launch states and authored chains may increase consequence;
- Guard/Poise reaching zero creates guard break, stagger or knockdown;
- Health reaching zero creates KO;
- self-damage, recoil and sacrifice exist only as named, visible authored mechanics;
- collision alone never invents damage ownership;
- every damaging event names the move and its consequence.

## Character law

A character is not a badge moving around the arena.

Each fighter requires:

- silhouette;
- stance;
- movement identity;
- role;
- move grammar;
- aim profile where relevant;
- defence/recovery behaviour;
- partner relation;
- voice/reaction identity;
- strengths, risks and counters visible through play.

## Arena law

The arena is a combatant and mechanic teacher, not a decorative rectangle or central puck board.

The first arena must provide:

- readable spatial lanes;
- corner and wall pressure;
- route choices;
- at least one interactive or destructible body;
- at least one risk/reward field condition;
- visual depth and world identity;
- a reason Bluefin and Crimson Gear are fighting there;
- a consequence for winning.

## Three-manner order

The three manners carry one combat truth.

1. **Clash Manual** proves full fighting depth.
2. **Clash Flow** translates locomotion/facing support without removing tactical ownership.
3. **Flick Clash** translates selected movements and attacks into drag/aim/flick enactment without reducing the fight to puck collisions.

> **Do not cut depth. Translate it into the hand mode that can carry it.**
