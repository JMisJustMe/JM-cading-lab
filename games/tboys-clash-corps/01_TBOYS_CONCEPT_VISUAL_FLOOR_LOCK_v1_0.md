## Absolute separation

Humanimals is a whole wide world in its own right. It is not a donor, comparison target, search lane or hidden influence for the present T-Boys production.

For this branch:

- do not search Humanimals;
- do not borrow Humanimals characters, world logic, story scale, visual anatomy or naming;
- do not use T-Boys as a technical rehearsal for Humanimals;
- do not treat Humanimals as a stronger version of T-Boys or T-Boys as a reduced Humanimals;
- preserve both as sovereign worlds with separate professional floors.

Theodore's current position is that T-Boys is fully certified for production, while Humanimals may carry far greater future storyline and character-design potential. That potential is protected by leaving it unopened here.

## What the T-Boys concept pictures establish

The supplied T-Boys concepts show a complete **toyetic competitive battle universe**, not an abstract physics board.

### Character floor

T-Boys fighters are:

- visible full-body characters;
- stylised, bold and toyetic;
- readable at phone scale;
- differentiated by silhouette, stance, colour, outfit, weapon/tool and motion identity;
- presented as named team members rather than anonymous tokens;
- capable of direct attacks, aimed attacks, defence, movement, reactions and team actions.

A badge, puck, polygon or floating initial cannot stand in for the professional fighter body.

### Battle floor

The pictures repeatedly show:

- opposing teams occupying and contesting the same live arena;
- visible attack, pass, launch, dodge, guard, combo and support routes;
- aim lines beginning from a fighter and resolving towards an opponent, ally, goal, wall, lane or objective;
- projectiles, powers, impacts and body reactions;
- tactical spacing and multiple simultaneous threats;
- attack and counter-action rather than collision-only damage;
- cards or abilities serving the battle rather than replacing it.

The result must read as **fighting for advantage**, even when the current mode uses sport, goal, fort, reflect or route mechanics.

### Arena floor

The T-Boys concepts establish multiple strong arena families:

- neon core chambers;
- sports courts and pitch-like fields;
- ruined or industrial battlefields;
- castle / fort spaces;
- reflective route arenas;
- objective and goal spaces;
- multi-lane tactical grounds.

Across these families, arenas contain:

- depth;
- lanes and routes;
- cover, walls, corners or structures;
- hazards and opportunities;
- team-side readability;
- a visible contested objective where appropriate;
- a world identity beyond a central puck and four bumpers.

The arena is a combatant and mechanic teacher.

### Camera and presentation floor

The concepts favour a tactical top-down / isometric battle view with enough closeness for character identity and enough height for route planning.

The camera may adapt by mode, but the first professional T-Boys battle must preserve:

- clear fighter silhouettes;
- readable facing and team position;
- visible attack and assist routes;
- enough arena context for tactical decisions;
- strong impact focus without losing the field;
- phone-first legibility.

### HUD floor

The pictures establish a game-native HUD rather than an engine dashboard:

- compact team portraits and health/state information;
- timer, score, round or objective status where relevant;
- energy / resource body;
- a small action-card or ability rail near the thumb zone;
- contextual prompts during aim, pass, guard, dodge or launch;
- centre playfield protected from permanent panels;
- strong colour separation between teams and active states.

### Mode-family floor

The supplied concepts visibly support a T-Boys universe containing distinct PvP expressions such as:

- Court Clash;
- Core Clash;
- Goal / Pass Clash;
- Reflect Clash;
- Fortified Forts;
- Fight Clash;
- Squad Clash;
- related sports, arena and objective clashes.

These are not all required in the first professional playable. They establish that T-Boys can support multiple fighting modes through one named-team universe.

The first production slice remains one complete 2v2 battle. Future modes must reuse proven fighters and combat organs without flattening each mode into the same arena with different labels.

## Current first-game interpretation

The professional first game should feel closest to a **direct 2v2 arena battle** with:

- full character control;
- close and ranged engagement;
- aimable attacks inside combat;
- active partner assists and tags;
- Guard / Poise and Health;
- knockback, stagger, knockdown, recovery and finish;
- one visually alive arena;
- clear Bluefin versus Crimson Gear rivalry;
- enough objective pressure to create movement without becoming air hockey.

The Route Core may survive only as a world organ, arena mechanism or temporary pressure condition. It may not dominate the game as a puck-like central object.

## Visualang requirements

Visualang must make the fight readable through:

- **shape / silhouette** — who and what the fighter is;
- **stance / facing** — readiness, target and vulnerability;
- **motion** — intent, weight, route and commitment;
- **colour** — team, energy, danger and state;
- **contact** — attack, block, evade, counter, assist or environmental interaction;
- **transformation** — damage, guard break, stagger, launch, knockdown, recovery or KO;
- **trace** — what move occurred, who caused it and what changed.

No hidden collision calculation may substitute for authored move meaning.

## Professional prohibitions

Do not deliver:

- abstract pieces presented as characters;
- an empty rectangular arena;
- central-object air hockey;
- passive partner portraits;
- collision-only attacks;
- arbitrary health loss;
- UI that is stronger than the game beneath it;
- cards before embodied combat;
- Humanimals material inside the T-Boys branch;
- another low-quality placeholder playable described as a professional step.

## Evidence boundary

The concept images are authoritative visual and directional source material. They are not evidence that every pictured mechanic has already been implemented, balanced or phone-tested.

The build must translate their strongest consistent qualities into a coherent, playable and technically proven T-Boys body rather than copying every panel into one overloaded first version.

## Keeper

> **T-Boys is a bright, toyetic, named-team fighting universe. Fighters carry the action, arenas create the pressure, aiming shapes selected combat routes, and every mode must still feel like opposition under contact. Humanimals stays outside the door.**
