## Owner contact verdict

`T-Boys Duo Circuit v0.1.2` has materially improved HUD clarity, movement readability and carrier stability, but it does **not** presently feel like battling or fighting.

The owner described it accurately as closer to a **confused air-hockey game**.

This verdict is accepted as the governing contact truth.

## Classification

`Duo Circuit v0.1.2` is preserved as:

- a carrier repair proof;
- an aiming-profile proof;
- a tag/assist experiment;
- a Visualang/HUD experiment;
- a combat-genre mismatch receipt.

It is **not** the full game base and must not be polished forward as though more effects or larger damage numbers will turn it into a fighter.

## Why the current body misses fighting

1. **Launched bodies are the main combat verb.** Movement and attack are collapsed into one hockey-like shove.
2. **Damage is inferred from collision.** Authored attacks, hit reactions, guard, stagger and move identity are weak or absent.
3. **Defence lacks embodiment.** There is no satisfying block, evade, parry, armour, counter or recovery exchange.
4. **Characters read as pieces.** The procedural icons lack full silhouettes, animation, facing, posture, attack language and emotional response.
5. **The arena reads as a board.** The Route Core and fixed circles dominate more than terrain, cover, distance, danger and character interaction.
6. **The battle story is thin.** Contact changes bars and labels but does not reliably create setup, pressure, interruption, reversal, knockdown and finish.
7. **The health basis is arbitrary.** HP and damage are not yet grounded in authored move data, defence state, hit quality, body zone or encounter condition.

## Correct genre body

The active target is:

> **A portrait-first 2v2 tag arena fighter with physics-enhanced aiming.**

Drag, aim, flick, rebound and route prediction remain important. They become **combat languages inside the game**, not the whole definition of combat.

## Full combat verbs

**MOVE → FACE → ENGAGE → ATTACK → DEFEND → EVADE → COUNTER → ASSIST → TAG → CHAIN → KNOCKDOWN → RECOVER → PRESSURE → FINISH**

Every fighter must support enough of these verbs to create a real exchange.

## Damage and health basis

### 1. Authored move damage

Damage comes from named moves with explicit:

- startup;
- active window;
- recovery;
- range;
- angle;
- hit zone;
- base damage;
- guard damage;
- poise/stagger damage;
- knockback;
- state application;
- cancel/chain permissions;
- partner interaction.

Passive body overlap does not cause ordinary combat damage.

### 2. Two-layer survival

Each fighter carries:

- **Guard / Poise** — short-term defence, balance and stagger resistance;
- **Health** — lasting body condition and KO route.

Guard can recover. Health normally does not fully recover during the round unless a named move or rule allows it.

### 3. Contact quality

A hit resolves as one of:

- graze;
- guarded hit;
- clean hit;
- counter hit;
- assist chain;
- wall/arena hit;
- launch/air hit;
- finisher.

The category changes damage, stagger, knockback and follow-up permission.

### 4. No hidden self-damage

Self-damage, recoil, sacrifice, armour break or shared-duo damage may exist only as named, visible character mechanics with an authored reason and readable Visualang consequence.

## Character body floor

The first four fighters must have:

- recognisable full-body silhouettes;
- facing and stance;
- idle, move, attack, hit, guard, knockdown, recovery and KO animation states;
- at least one close attack;
- at least one aimed/ranged or route-shaped attack;
- defence or evasion;
- assist action;
- tag action;
- signature mechanic;
- duo relation;
- voice/reaction identity;
- a reason to fight.

Aero, Nova, Raze and Grit may be retained only after being rebuilt to this character floor. Their current icon bodies are reference markers, not final fighters.

## Arena body floor

The first arena must provide:

- traversable combat space rather than a board layout;
- readable foreground/background separation;
- walls, cover, elevation, lanes or changing routes;
- at least one interactable or destructible body;
- one arena hazard or opportunity;
- one positional advantage route;
- wall impact and corner-pressure consequence;
- visual/world reason for the battle.

The Route Core may survive as an arena organ only if it supports combat. It cannot visually or mechanically turn the match back into puck control.

## Control manners

### Clash Manual

Direct movement, facing, defence, attack and partner control.

### Clash Flow

One-hand assisted locomotion and facing while the player still owns target, timing, attack choice, defence, assist and tag.

### Flick Clash

Drag/aim/flick shapes selected attacks, dashes, launches, grapples, projectiles or finishers. It does not force every movement and every attack to become a body-launch collision.

All three manners carry the same combat truth.

This is a **full narrow game**, not a broad engine demo.
