E. Interaction Field Test

Use this on any idea.

1. What are the interactors?
Who or what is acting?

2. What is the field?
Where does the interaction happen?

3. What pressure enters?
Touch, speech, force, emotion, command, movement, choice or mistake?

4. What responds?
Person, object, rule, system, environment, body or AI?

5. What changes?
Position, meaning, emotion, score, state, route, confidence or memory?

6. What trace remains?
Evidence, habit, mark, memory, result, saved state or new rule?

7. What can happen next because of it?
What door opens?

This is the practical engine.

F. First Interactor Cards

Card 001 — The Player

Type: Human interactor.
Field: Game, app, world, conversation or tool.
Input: Touch, choice, speech, movement or pressure.
Changes field by: Acting.
Changed by field through: Feedback, consequence, resistance and reward.
Trace: Skill, memory, progress, frustration or mastery.

Card 002 — The Rule

Type: Invisible interactor.
Field: Game, system, relationship or environment.
Input: Trigger condition.
Changes field by: Deciding what happens next.
Changed by field through: Exceptions, updates, pressure and redesign.
Trace: Pattern, expectation, fairness or unfairness.

Card 003 — The Wall

Type: Boundary interactor.
Field: Game arena, social boundary or body limit.
Input: Contact.
Changes field by: Stopping, redirecting, rebounding or warning.
Changed by field through: Damage, marking, opening or breaking.
Trace: Boundary memory.

GlyphPlay translation:

A wall is not decoration. A wall is an interactor because contact with it changes what can happen.

Card 004 — The AI

Type: Responsive interactor.
Field: Chat, engine, tool or assistant.
Input: Prompt, correction, examples, pressure and preference.
Changes field by: Generating, translating, structuring and responding.
Changed by field through: User correction, context and constraints.
Trace: Better output, worse drift, preserved pattern or product body.

Card 005 — The Body

Type: Living interactor.
Field: World, stress, speech, movement and attention.
Input: Breath, posture, pressure, hunger, rhythm, pain and energy.
Changes field by: Participating or withdrawing.
Changed by field through: Load, support, environment and recovery.
Trace: Tightness, release, habit and capacity.

G. Three Demonstrations

Demo 1 — Game

A dot moves toward a wall.

Old view: Dot hits wall.

Interactor view:

Dot enters field → wall receives contact → rule responds → dot rebounds → field state changes → player learns wall behaviour.

Product use: This becomes a game mechanic.

Demo 2 — AI Chat

A user says: “This is too abstract.”

AI responds by simplifying.

Old view: AI rewrote text.

Interactor view:

User pressure entered the field → AI changed route → output became more usable → trust increased or decreased depending on result.

Product use: This becomes an AI collaboration model.

Demo 3 — Relationship

One person says something sharply. The other withdraws.

Old view: Argument.

Interactor view:

Tone entered field → body interpreted threat → trust state changed → communication route closed → repair route needed.

Product use: This becomes a communication recovery tool.
